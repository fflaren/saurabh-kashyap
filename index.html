§ 01The Method

Most numbers in a business
agree with each other. The useful work is in the ones that don't.

Between April 2025 and June 2026, across two companies and two mandates, the work took the same shape every time. Take the plan. Take the actual. Measure the distance. Decompose it until a driver has a name — a mis-allocated overhead, a component priced above market, a run-rate nobody re-based. Then close it before the review cycle ends.

Six business units. Five cost centres. Five product groups. Four verticals. Three product lines. A fourteen-component bill of materials. The deviations that came out of them were 8% and 9% — both isolated, both traced to their cost drivers, both closed inside the cycle they surfaced in.
